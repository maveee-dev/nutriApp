Highest-Impact Reusable Grammar Rules
-------------------------------------
1. state-and-preparation-as-variants — 4764 foods (58.41%)
   Keep raw/cooked/frozen/dried and grilled/roasted/fried descriptors secondary unless they define a distinct dish.
   Examples: Abiyuch, raw | Acerola juice, raw | Acerola, (west indian cherry), raw | Agave, cooked (Southwest) | Agave, dried (Southwest)

2. animal-plus-cut — 2548 foods (31.24%)
   Promote an animal and cut together, while leaving state and preparation descriptors in variantLabel.
   Examples: APPLEBEE'S, 9 oz house sirloin steak | APPLEBEE'S, chicken tenders platter | APPLEBEE'S, chicken tenders, from kids' menu | ARBY'S, roast beef sandwich, classic | BANQUET, Salisbury Steak With Gravy, family size, frozen, unprepared

3. beverage-type-first — 846 foods (10.37%)
   Promote the beverage type or product instead of Alcoholic Beverage or Beverages as the title.
   Examples: Acerola juice, raw | Alcoholic Beverage, wine, table, red, Barbera | Alcoholic Beverage, wine, table, red, Burgundy | Alcoholic Beverage, wine, table, red, Cabernet Franc | Alcoholic Beverage, wine, table, red, Cabernet Sauvignon

4. fish-species-first — 176 foods (2.16%)

Interpretation
--------------
Counts represent foods whose canonical names contain each normalized term at least once.
They are planning signals for reusable grammar, not automatic approval to promote a term into displayName.
Quality, size, state, preparation, brand, and category terms should generally remain variantLabel candidates unless the grammar rule establishes that they define the food concept.
